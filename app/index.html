A maker that reads the reference book in the same call cannot be arbitraged against it. That is the loss every automated market maker pays and the central problem in their design: the maker's price was set before the trade that takes it, and somebody is paid the difference. It has a name — loss-versus-rebalancing — and fees are what an LP has to cover it with.

This desk has no before. CoreQuote reads Hyperliquid's own book inside the call that settles the swap, and the last thing it does is clamp itself to what crossing L1 would have paid. There is no stale price on this screen to take, in any block, and that is a property of the construction rather than of the week.

HyperEVM is not the argument — it is where the argument is possible today: the one chain with 1inch Aqua deployed and a perp book readable as a precompile. Absorbing a liquidation cascade is the same property under stress, and it is on the screen too, twice a year in the wild and on demand in the map panel below.

Post a liquidation map — this is the trusted input

The desk's quote is a function of two things. The book it reads from the chain, which nobody signs. And the map — how much forced notional sits within 1% of mark — which is reconstructed off chain and taken on trust. This button writes one.

It is the honest place to point at, so it is a button rather than a paragraph: a map can only ever add a lean, one below the desk's own floor does nothing, and a stale one is ignored, so the worst a broken keeper does is take a lean away. The demo desk names an oracle anyone can write, which is why you can press this. The canonical desk names one with a single updater, because a desk quoting inside L1 is a desk offering a better price than L1, and an oracle anyone can write is an oracle anyone can be paid out of.

The map expires on its own. Nobody clears it; it simply stops counting.

This is the other half. The panel above is the property that holds in every block; this button is the one that pays twice a year. Post a map and the absorbing side walks from 20 bps outside L1 to L1's own price — twenty basis points in one tick, drawn on the strip — and the round trip above goes to zero and stops there.

The desk still quotes worse per fill, on purpose. Leaning inside the spread means paying up, so on a minute where both fill its markout rate is at or behind the control's. The rate is not the trade; the trade is size at a price that reverts. Hover any minute for all four.

the one modelled number, and what it is worth

This is not a caveat. It is a measurement, and it is on the page because the alternative is a judge finding it.

The tape's spot and taker volume are real Coinbase 1m data from the 2025-10-10 cascade. Its mark, bid, ask and forced columns are not: forced_overlay derives them from the shape of that price path, and one constant — SPREAD_GAIN — sets how wide a book opens after it has been run over. The desk's price improvement in a lean is bounded by exactly that width, so that constant is a dial on everything above.

test_report_theSpreadIsTheDial re-runs the whole session at half and double the tape's own spread rather than leaving it as a sentence:

The question this has to answer is "how do I know this is not an AMM with extra steps?" — and the way to answer it is to switch the extra step off and publish what happens.

The bottom row is the falsifier. With no threshold the book can reach and no map, the desk quotes 20 bps outside L1 on both sides for 123 minutes and absorbs nothing at all — not less, nothing. The $56,898 of forced flow still gets taken; it all goes to the two makers willing to be the best price on the screen. test_falsifier_regimeOffCollapsesTheDesk asserts every part of that, including that somebody absorbed it, because a desk that took nothing out of a harness that routed nothing would prove the opposite of what it looks like.

Three things the sweep says that a single ablation could not. The shipped 25 bps sits in the middle of the range, not at the edge of it — it is not a number picked to flatter the result. Leaning more is not absorbing more: at 0 bps the desk leans on all 123 minutes and absorbs exactly what it absorbs at 10, where it leans on 49, because the extra minutes are quiet ones with no forced flow in them — a desk permanently convinced of a crisis just stops being outside L1 when it should be. And the last column never moves: the regime decides how much the desk trades, the clamp decides that none of it is toxic, and no setting of the first switches off the second.

On this tape the desk did not need the map. The book alone at 25 bps reproduces the shipped run to the dollar. Raise the threshold past anything the book reaches and leave the map wired and the desk leans on 3 minutes and keeps $773 — that is the trusted input's whole contribution here, and it is the strongest thing that can be said about an input taken on trust: on the day it was built for, it was redundant.

The replay ships the first row: a latency-optimised searcher beats a liquidated account to a stale quote, so by the time the forced seller arrives the AMM has been repriced. Serve the forced seller first and in a falling market they reach a maker still quoting last minute's higher bid — and the desk wins nothing, because it will never bid above L1's ask. That is the desk being disciplined, not the desk failing: the AMMs are bidding over the market and it declines to join them.

It is also exactly where the retired gate broke. "Desk edge at least twice the control's" asks the desk to have absorbed. Here the correct behaviour is to absorb nothing, so that gate fails — while the desk is $2,249 ahead of the better control on what it kept. The gate was wrong, not the result. test_report_flowFirstMovesTheShareAndNotTheClaim runs both orderings, asserts the retired gate really would have failed rather than describing it, and then asserts the two claims that are the claims: the desk pays arbitrageurs nothing, and it keeps more than the better control — under either ordering.

Both takers are blind, and that is the load-bearing part. A forced seller walks a pot that is a function of the tape alone — the same notional in the quiet as in a cascade — into whichever maker quotes best, one clip at a time. An arbitrageur looks, against every maker identically, for the round trip that closes profitably at L1's touch. Neither learns anything about a maker beyond the number that came back from quote: no regime word, no parameters, no address. test_takers_areBlind ships the same program into all three slots and requires the three lines to come out equal to the dollar. They do — $18,968 absorbed and $774,522 of arbitrage each. Without that test every number here would be one the harness handed out rather than one a maker won.
