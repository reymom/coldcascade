post a liquidation map — the one trusted input, by hand

The quote is a function of two things: the book it reads from the chain, which nobody signs, and the map — how much forced notional sits near mark — which is reconstructed off chain and taken on trust. A map can only ever add a lean; one below the desk's own floor does nothing, and a stale one is ignored, so the worst a broken keeper does is take a lean away. The button above the strip fires this same write with no choices; this fold keeps the controls. The demo desk names an oracle anyone can write, which is why you can press it; the canonical desk names one with a single updater.

what the bound is, and what it is not

The charge the sentence names is the loss every automated market maker pays and the central problem in their design: the maker's price was set before the trade that takes it, and somebody is paid the difference. This desk has no before. CoreQuote reads Hyperliquid's own book inside the call that settles the swap, and the last thing it does is clamp itself to what crossing the book would have paid. So there is no earlier price on this screen to take — against the book that quote just read, in the block it settled in.

The bound holds against the book the quote read, on one instrument, at one instant. What it leaves standing is ordinary inventory risk: somebody who is right about the next move still wins here, with no round trip on this screen ever paying. Absorbing a liquidation cascade is the same property under stress — twice a year in the wild, on demand from the button above.

the book archive — what the book was

Hyperliquid's book reaches HyperEVM through precompiles that ignore the block tag: asking for the past returns the present, without an error. So what was the BBO at this instant is not a slow query, it is not a query at all — unless somebody wrote it down while it was true. BookCache.poke is that writing: four uint64, about once a minute, into an EVM log.

how an answer is built — and why it is never a number

Ask for an instant and the panel returns the two observations that bracket it, each with its distance — never a number, never interpolated. It is the same answer the MCP server gives, from the same file, with the commands that reproduce it underneath.

          the four lines, and what they are not
          
The line to beat is the dashed one, not the grey ones. A zero-fee
            constant product is the right ablation — it is the desk program with one instruction
            removed, so a gap between them is that instruction and nothing else — and it is nobody's
            idea of a competitor. The amber line is the same curve charging 30 bps through 1inch's own
            FlatFeeIn, which is what people actually deploy. The dashed line is
            Hyperliquid itself: the identical forced flow, filled at L1's own bid and ask, holding no
            inventory and never arbitraged.
            


            The desk clears the venue on a smaller book. It ends above L1's touch while
            absorbing a third less notional — 355 bps per dollar against 215 — because it is in the
            seven minutes that paid and absent from every quiet one.
            


            The desk still quotes worse per fill, on purpose. Leaning inside the spread means
            paying up, so on a minute where both fill its markout rate is at or behind the
            control's. The rate is not the trade; the trade is size at a price that reverts. Hover any
            minute for all four.
            


            This page is about price. There is no perp leg in it. The edge is the markout of
            inventory each maker kept. That does not sum with a hedge: buy at P,
            short at H, mark both at M, and the combined result is
            q × (H − P), because the rebound the markout measures is exactly what a hedge
            gives up. The cover leg is a different measurement and it lives on chain 999.
            


            There is no multiple on this page because the honest denominator does not exist: both
            AMM lines lose money on what they absorbed, which is what a maker whose price was set
            before the trade does in a cascade. A zero and a pair of signed numbers, never a ratio.

      the one modelled number, and what it is worth
      
This is not a caveat. It is a measurement, and it is on the page because the
        alternative is a judge finding it.

      
The tape's spot and taker volume are real Coinbase 1m data from
        the 2025-10-10 cascade. Its mark, bid, ask and forced
        columns are not: forced_overlay derives them from the shape of that price
        path, and one constant — SPREAD_GAIN — sets how wide a book opens after it has
        been run over. The desk's price improvement in a lean is bounded by exactly that width, so
        that constant is a dial on everything above.

      how these numbers were produced, and why the takers are blind
      
Reads results/oct10_replay.csv, written by
        forge test --match-contract Oct10Replay, whose 45 columns are documented in
        results/oct10_replay.schema.md. Three makers are shipped into Aqua and every
        fill on this page settles through 1inch's official router.
        


        Both takers are blind, and that is the load-bearing part. A forced seller walks a pot
        that is a function of the tape alone — the same notional in the quiet as in a cascade — into
        whichever maker quotes best, one clip at a time. An arbitrageur looks, against every maker
        identically, for the round trip that closes profitably at L1's touch. Neither learns
        anything about a maker beyond the number that came back from quote: no regime
        word, no parameters, no address. test_takers_areBlind ships the same program
        into all three slots and requires the three lines to come out equal to the dollar.
        They do — $18,968 absorbed and $774,522 of arbitrage notional each. Without that test every number
        here would be one the harness handed out rather than one a maker won.
        


        results/oct10_replay.source carries each line's session totals and the hash of
        the tape they came from.

One measurement is kept out of the check's way. A request Privy
        still has to populate gets its gas estimated first, so calldata the desk would revert on
        comes back as transaction_broadcast_failure before the policy has said
        anything — which reads exactly like a policy that allowed it. Every case above is
        either fully populated or uses calldata that does not revert.

        is not enforced at all on a wallet whose owner_id is null, and a policy whose
        own owner_id is null can be rewritten by anything holding the app secret. An
        owned wallet under an unowned policy is a lock with its key beside it.

    


    

      the cover — a hedge on the same venue, in a second transaction
      
cover is the hedge a desk's owner has authorised. cover()
        is a separate transaction, sent by the owner or by an operator the owner names, and it
        queues an order: CoreWriter hands it to HyperCore, which executes it seconds later and
        may reject or partially fill it without failing the transaction that carried it. A fill
        is read back off HyperCore. The desks that used it are the two above.
